The food of the kings of the Peruvian Empire

Pachamanca

The archived site described a meal of beef, chicken, pork, potatoes, corn on the cob, dessert, ají, and salad. Pachamanca is the Quechua name for an ancient cooking method associated with the Incas.

At high elevations, thin air and a lower boiling temperature can make cooking slow. The pachamanca oven addressed this by trapping heat and pressure underground while giving the food a distinctive flavor.

How the earth oven was prepared

  1. A small hole was dug in the earth and lined with rocks, forming a structure resembling an igloo.
  2. A fire heated the stones for approximately one and a half to two hours.
  3. The heated stones were removed, and potatoes, sweet potatoes, corn, and marinated meats were placed inside.
  4. Hot stones were returned and intermixed with the food. Desserts such as sweet cheese, pumpkin with brown sugar, or humitas were placed last.
  5. Large green leaves and several inches of earth sealed the oven while the food roasted.
Pacha = earth or dirt
Manca = kettle

The original text noted that while the food cooked, guests socialized and enjoyed chicha or beer before the pachamanca was uncovered and served.
