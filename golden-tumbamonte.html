Music, dancing, and community

Chonguinada & Tumbamonte

The original website described the tumbamonte as an afternoon dance held after the meal and volleyball. Friends, relatives, and guests danced around a tree decorated with candy.

The Tumbamonte tradition

A designated “godfather” brought an axe, and each dancer received a turn chopping the decorated tree. After each strike, the dancer was traditionally offered a drink of pisco, a Peruvian brandy. The godfather managed both the pisco and the axe for safety.

The person whose strike brought down the tree became the godfather for the following year. When the tree fell, the children rushed forward to collect the candy.

The chonguinada is a traditional Peruvian dance associated with the central Andes. On the archived Golden Pachamanca site, it appeared alongside the tumbamonte as part of the gathering’s cultural entertainment.
