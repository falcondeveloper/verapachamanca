History of the Golden Pachamanca

The Golden Pachamanca was held annually by Dick Thompson and the Vera family. Dick Thompson and Sam Vera became friends in the 1970s.

Sam Vera moved from Peru to Denver in 1958, followed by his wife and children. The family then moved to Leadville, where Sam worked as a mining engineer for the Climax Molybdenum Mine. The local tradition began in 1960, when Sam and Dina Vera and their children celebrated their first pachamanca with friends and associates from the mine.

For the next sixteen years, the Veras continued holding annual pachamancas and introducing Peruvian cooking to people in Leadville and Climax.

Major gatherings

  • 1972: Climax inaugurated a major open-pit molybdenum mine with a pachamanca for approximately 480 invited executives, staff members, and managers.
  • 1975: The Veras prepared a pachamanca for approximately 380 students, professors, friends, and guests during E-Day at the Colorado School of Mines.
  • 1976: Sam Vera retired from Climax and moved to Golden, where many close friends and relatives lived.

Sam Vera died in the fall of 2002, and Isabel Thompson died in 2001. The archived history remembers both of them for working tirelessly to make the pachamanca a special gathering.
